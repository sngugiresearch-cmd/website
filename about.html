About

Mechanistic interpretability researcher — Nairobi, Kenya.

I'm Stanley, a researcher fascinated by how artificial intelligence actually works. Instead of treating neural networks as mysterious black boxes, I dig into the mechanics — figuring out how they represent knowledge and make decisions at the most fundamental level.

My path into AI research was unconventional: intensive self-directed study combined with hands-on experimentation, always starting from first principles. I don't just want to know that something works — I need to understand why. That has taken me from advanced mathematics through transformer architectures to the internals of open-weight LLMs.

// Research focus

My work centers on mechanistic interpretability — reverse-engineering how AI systems think. Concretely:

  • Knowledge editing — precisely rewriting what models know without breaking everything else (arXiv 2508.07075)
  • Cross-lingual representations — how low-resource languages like Swahili live inside multilingual models (arXiv 2506.15415)
  • Polysemanticity & features — what makes learned features entangle, and how training choices shape it (blog post)

My methodology combines human insight with AI collaboration — I lead the conceptual work while leveraging AI tools for synthesis and implementation. This lets me move fast while maintaining complete understanding and accountability for everything I create.

// Beyond research

Living in Kenya gives me a unique perspective on AI development from the Global South, which shapes how I think about accessibility and the global impact of these technologies. On this blog I write about interpretability experiments, model internals, and what the field is actually discovering under the hype.

Always up for discussing research, collaborating, or comparing notes on interpretability. The most interesting discoveries happen when different perspectives collide.
